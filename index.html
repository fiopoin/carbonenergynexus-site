Power and data centers

US data centers have announced more than 115 GW of their own gas plants, enough to add 152 Mt of CO2 a year

Speed to power is pulling AI campuses off the grid. Utilities are answering with long contracts and big upfront charges, while drought maps show where cooling water could run short.

More than 100 behind-the-meter gas plants had been announced at US data centers by October 2026, totaling over 115 GW, more than double California's peak demand, MIT Technology Review reported Nov 24, citing Cleanview. About 4.9 GW is operating and 15.8 GW is under construction. BloombergNEF counts 181 GW of on-site data center gas announced worldwide, 90% of it in the US, and estimates the planned US plants could emit 345 million tonnes of CO2e a year by 2035, 152 million tonnes more than drawing the same power from the grid.

Utilities are pricing that load in. TVA disclosed $1.7 billion in aggregate payments from one unnamed data center customer under its new large-load rate, which charges about $1.5 million per MW of requested capacity above 5 MW, Utility Dive reported Nov 24. In North Carolina, regulators now require qualifying data centers to sign contracts of at least 10 years, pay demand charges on at least 75% of contracted capacity and post guarantees against stranded costs, WRAL reported.

Water is the next constraint. A BMI note published Nov 24 found that about 21% of 83.6 GW of tracked global data center capacity sits where El Niño raises water stress, with 14.9 GW highly exposed, 92% of it in Asia Pacific. In Nevada, a Desert Research Institute study estimates proposed data centers could use about 12,770 acre feet a year for cooling by 2036, plus 21,283 acre feet indirectly through power generation.

Sources: MIT Technology Review, Nov 24, 2026; Utility Dive, Nov 24, 2026; WRAL, Nov 24, 2026; TNGlobal, Nov 24, 2026; Facilities Dive, Nov 20, 2026; Utility Dive, Nov 23, 2026; Carbon Herald, Nov 24, 2026

Through the nexus

Electrons

Grid operators face load that may never connect. TVA's large-load tariff and North Carolina's 75% minimum demand charge shift that risk onto the data center customer.

Molecules

Gas wins on speed. Mitsubishi Heavy Industries booked 8 GW of large gas turbine orders in the first half of its fiscal year and holds a 37 GW backlog, Utility Dive reported.

Pore space

Capturing that CO2 would need storage wells. EPA has permitted 18 Class VI wells across 10 projects, with applications for more than 100 wells pending, the Carbon Capture Coalition said in reform proposals filed this month.

Water

BMI found drought in 25% to 37% of El Niño months since 2013 across the Johor, Singapore and Batam cluster, which has 6.6 GW of data center capacity live or planned.

Use it

Investors
On-site gas buys speed but carries fuel and carbon exposure. Check who holds the stranded cost if a campus stalls.
Regulators
Minimum demand charges and exit guarantees are spreading. Compare TVA and North Carolina terms before setting your own.
Industry
Turbine slots are scarce. Lock in equipment, water and permits together, not in sequence.
Founders
Firm, low carbon power near load and dry cooling are clear gaps. Buyers want delivery dates, not pilots.

The brief

Capital

Porthos budget rises to as much as €1.7 billion and start up slips to 2028

The Rotterdam CO2 transport and storage project now expects costs to rise by up to €397 million, including a risk buffer, from a €1.3 billion baseline at its investment decision, Porthos said Nov 19. Work will finish at the end of 2027 at the earliest, with a realistic possibility of operation in 2028 after testing. It blamed slower work on the offshore platform and compressor station and contractors staying on longer. Separately, Equinor chief executive Anders Opedal said delays at European capture plants leave very little CO2 available for new storage contracts, Briefs Finance reported Nov 24, citing Bloomberg.

Emitters
If your capture plant depends on Porthos, plan for a 2028 start and check delay clauses.
Investors
Storage cost overruns and thin CO2 supply are now the two risks to price in European CCS.

Sources: Porthos, Nov 19, 2026; Briefs Finance, Nov 24, 2026

Markets

China will join the airline carbon offset scheme CORSIA for 2027 to 2029, keeping a veto on credits

The Civil Aviation Administration of China said Nov 23 it will join CORSIA voluntarily and in an orderly manner for 2027 to 2029, and offsets used by Chinese airlines will need government approval, Carbon Herald reported. ICAO has approved four crediting programs for the period. IATA estimates airlines may need 200 to 250 million eligible units by January 2028, well above supply. Separately, the EU and UK concluded talks on Nov 20 to link their emissions trading systems from Jan 1, 2028, subject to ratification, Rigzone reported.

Credit developers
CORSIA eligible supply is short. Check whether your methodology and host country authorization qualify.
Airlines
Price 2027 to 2029 compliance now; Chinese approval rules could narrow the pool.

Sources: Carbon Herald, Nov 24, 2026; Rigzone, Nov 24, 2026

Regulation

Carbon capture groups ask EPA for 12 month Class VI permit reviews and area permits

The Carbon Capture Coalition sent EPA recommendations to improve the Class VI program, Carbon Herald reported Nov 24. It asks for a 12 month window to finish permit reviews, area permits that cover several wells in one storage complex, standard emergency response protocols and a definition of the CO2 stream that explicitly includes direct air capture. EPA has permitted 18 Class VI wells across 10 projects, and applications for more than 100 wells are pending. Seven states now run their own Class VI programs, including Colorado from November.

Developers
Area permits would change how you sequence wells. Model both single and area permit timelines.
State regulators
Primacy states set the pace. Track Utah's pending application and Colorado's first decisions.

Sources: Carbon Herald, Nov 24, 2026

Markets

EU carbon posts its sharpest weekly drop in nine months after an ETS vote stalls

The December 2026 EU allowance fell 7.8% on the week to €81.26 a tonne and briefly touched €80.03, the lowest since late July, before rising almost 3% to about €83.6 on Monday, cbam.news reported Nov 24. Weekly volume reached 183 million allowances against a 2026 average of 133 million. On Nov 19 the European Parliament's industry committee rejected a compromise on the post 2030 ETS review and postponed its vote, EU Perspectives reported. The provisional fourth quarter CBAM certificate price was €86.69.

Compliance buyers
The dip came without compliance demand. Consider hedging before the Dec 14 final auction of the year.
Importers
CBAM costs track EUA prices. Use the €86.69 provisional figure for fourth quarter budgets.

Sources: cbam.news, Nov 24, 2026; EU Perspectives, Nov 19, 2026

Water

El Niño puts 14.9 GW of data center capacity at high water risk, most of it in Asia Pacific

BMI found that about 21% of 83.6 GW of tracked global data center capacity sits where El Niño raises water stress, TNGlobal reported Nov 24. Some 14.9 GW across 16 locations is highly exposed, 92% of it in Asia Pacific, and 12.4 GW faces both drought and higher cooling demand. Bangkok and Chonburi saw drought in 44% of El Niño months against 5% in neutral periods. Daily highs could rise 0.5 to 1.1 degrees C in Hyderabad, Bangkok, Jakarta and Ho Chi Minh City.

Developers
Run site water plans against El Niño years, not averages.
Utilities
Expect cooling demand and water stress to peak together in the same months.

Sources: TNGlobal, Nov 24, 2026

Projects

New York targets 5 GW of new nuclear as India restarts Kaiga 1

New York released an advanced nuclear master plan targeting 5 GWe of new capacity upstate, with the New York Power Authority planning at least 1 GWe, World Nuclear News reported Nov 23. In India, Kaiga 1 reached criticality on Nov 22 after about 600 days offline for coolant channel replacement, and the regulator cleared the 700 MWe RAPP 8 unit in Rajasthan for first criticality on Nov 19. Korea's K-Sure and Slovakia's Eximbanka signed a memorandum on co-financing power projects, including nuclear.

Suppliers
New York's developer list is set. Position for fuel, components and grid work.
Lenders
Export credit pacts like K-Sure and Eximbanka are becoming the template for nuclear finance.

Sources: World Nuclear News, Nov 23, 2026; World Nuclear News, Nov 24, 2026; World Nuclear News, Nov 23, 2026

Critical minerals and mining

China buys two more months of calm on gallium, germanium, antimony and graphite, while copper gives back its AI rally and lithium gets a new futures contract in Guangzhou.

§Minerals policy

China extends its reprieve on US bound gallium, germanium and antimony to Jan 27

China's Commerce Ministry on Nov 24 extended its suspension of the ban on exports of gallium, germanium, antimony and superhard materials to the US until Jan 27, 2027, along with the pause on stricter end use reviews for graphite. The suspension had been due to end Nov 27. The ban on dual use items for US military users stays in force.

Sources: IBTimes, Nov 24, 2026; Reuters via Media Selangor, Nov 24, 2026

NdRare earths

NdPr oxide holds near a monthly high as Lynas meets shareholders without a permanent CEO

Chinese Pr-Nd oxide traded at 725,000 to 727,000 yuan a tonne on Nov 19, up from 715,000 to 718,000 a week earlier, SMM said. Lynas held its AGM on Nov 25 with an interim chief executive; the board's global search continues after Amanda Lacaze retired June 30. USA Rare Earth fell 5% to $6.69 on Nov 24, while MP Materials held at $35.69.

Sources: SMM, Nov 19, 2026; Lynas AGM transcript, Nov 25, 2026; 24/7 Wall St., Nov 24, 2026

CuCopper

Copper drops 5% back below $12,000 as the AI rally fades

LME three month copper fell 5.05% to $11,858.50 a tonne on Nov 24, erasing Monday's jump after Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang, Fastmarkets reported. Reuters had it at $11,790.50 late in the day, the weakest since March 23. Tin fell below $40,000 for the first time since December.

Sources: Fastmarkets, Nov 24, 2026; Reuters via Business Recorder, Nov 25, 2026

LiLithium

Guangzhou launches lithium hydroxide futures on Nov 26 as SQM warns on prices

The Guangzhou Futures Exchange starts lithium hydroxide futures on Nov 26 and options on Nov 27, its second lithium contract after carbonate, with first deliveries from June 2027, SMM reported. SQM shares fell 3.78% to $46.88 on Nov 24 after it warned fourth quarter prices could be substantially lower than the third.

Sources: SMM, Nov 13, 2026; Wisevoter, Nov 25, 2026

CoCobalt

Congo's export halt starts Nov 30 after shipments fell by more than half

Congo exported 61,441 tonnes of cobalt in the first nine months of 2026, against more than 145,000 tonnes in the same period of 2024, Miningmx reported Nov 24. The regulator will suspend exports from Nov 30 until further notice, citing a significant and sustained price decline since June. CMOC, Glencore and ERG hold the largest quotas.

Sources: Miningmx, Nov 24, 2026

UUranium

US uranium output falls 78% as the only conventional mill runs rare earths

US production fell to 243,673 pounds of U3O8 in the third quarter from 1,087,504 in the first, as Energy Fuels' White Mesa mill in Utah processed rare earths instead, World Nuclear News reported Nov 24. A December campaign should yield about 1 million pounds before the mill returns to rare earths through much of 2027.

Sources: World Nuclear News, Nov 24, 2026
