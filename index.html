Critical minerals

Congo will halt cobalt exports again from Nov 30 as prices slide and stockpiles build

The world's largest cobalt supplier is swapping its quota for a full suspension with no end date, to be reviewed after four months. In northern Thailand, arsenic linked to mining across the border has more than 20,000 households on trucked water.

Congo's strategic minerals regulator, ARECOMS, will suspend all cobalt exports from Nov 30, 2026, until further notice, SMM reported on Nov 23, citing Bloomberg. The decision was adopted at a Nov 9 meeting and covers industrial and artisanal mines, The Northern Miner reported. The regulator cited a sharp fall in prices since June, slower consumption growth, rising inventories and speculation, and said it will reassess after four months and revise how export quotas are allocated in the meantime.

It is Congo's second halt. The country supplied about three quarters of the world's mined cobalt before it first stopped shipments in February 2025, then switched to quotas in October 2025 with a 2026 ceiling of 96,600 tonnes. Cobalt hydroxide in Asia was $14.50 a pound of contained cobalt on Oct 14, down 43% since July 1, the Cobalt Institute said, citing Benchmark data. China's CMOC produced 99,694 tonnes of cobalt in the first nine months of 2026 but sold 12,661 tonnes, while Glencore's output fell by more than half to 13,700 tonnes.

Supply controls are tightening elsewhere too. Thailand plans to stop exporting chemicals used in rare earth mining to Myanmar after river contamination in its north, Reuters reported Nov 23. Vietnam broke ground the same day on a $60 million plant in Hai Phong to separate 10,000 tonnes a year of rare earth oxides from late 2028. And USA Rare Earth told the SEC that Brazil's new critical minerals law, whose rules are not yet written, could affect its Serra Verde operations.

Sources: SMM, Nov 23, 2026; The Northern Miner, Nov 23, 2026; Reuters via Mining.com.au, Nov 23, 2026; Al Jazeera, Nov 18, 2026; TNGlobal, Nov 23, 2026; The Rio Times, Nov 23, 2026; SMM, Nov 24, 2026; Industrial Info Resources, Oct 19, 2026

The brief

Capital

Niron plans a $1.8 billion rare earth free magnet complex in Alabama as rare earth stocks rebound

Niron Magnetics plans to invest $1.8 billion in a permanent magnet complex in Dothan, Alabama, its second site, SMM reported Nov 24. Construction is expected to begin in 2028, with production from 2029 at 10,000 tonnes a year of iron nitride magnets, an alternative to neodymium magnets, for AI, automotive, defence and industrial buyers; about 700 jobs are expected. Rare earth stocks rallied with the wider market on Monday: USA Rare Earth rose 13% to $7.09 and MP Materials 6% to $35.98 in afternoon trading, 24/7 Wall St. reported, though both remain down for the year. On its Nov 5 call MP said it does not expect today's elevated prices for several medium and heavy rare earths to persist indefinitely.

Investors
A 2028 construction start means years of execution risk. Weigh it against magnet price floors in existing offtakes.
Motor makers
Ask for test data on iron nitride performance in your duty cycle before planning a switch.

Sources: SMM, Nov 24, 2026; 24/7 Wall St., Nov 23, 2026

Water

Thailand moves to cut off mining chemicals to Myanmar as arsenic reaches its taps

Thailand plans to stop exporting chemicals used in rare earth mining to Myanmar amid cross border pollution, Deputy Prime Minister Yodchanan Wongsawat said on Saturday, Reuters reported. The government also plans to designate the Thai side of the Kok River a pollution control zone. Authorities are supplying clean water to more than 20,000 households after arsenic in Mae Sai district's waterworks reached 30 times the safe limit and untreated Sai River water 25 times the drinking water limit, Al Jazeera and AFP reported Nov 18. Researchers at Naresuan University traced much of the Sai River's arsenic laden sediment to gold mining in Myanmar. A joint Thai and Myanmar working group meets on Nov 25 and 26, The Nation reported, and Reuters said Thailand, China and Myanmar plan talks from Nov 24 to 26.

Magnet and battery buyers
Ask suppliers whether feedstock traces to Shan or Kachin State mines. Pollution claims are becoming a trade issue.
Water utilities
Upstream mining can outrun treatment. Arsenic monitoring and backup sources are the near term response.

Sources: Reuters via Mining.com.au, Nov 23, 2026; Al Jazeera, Nov 18, 2026; The Nation, Nov 17, 2026; The Nation, Nov 17, 2026

Projects

California offers up to $18 million for geothermal exploration and drilling

California will make up to $18 million available through the California Energy Commission for geothermal exploration and drilling, with a competitive solicitation opening in January 2027, the state said Nov 23. Three to four projects are expected to win $4.5 million to $6 million each, and applicants must match at least 100% of the state money, so at least $36 million in total is expected. The Department of Conservation is putting $1.1 million into resource mapping and will start an overhaul of geothermal well rules in early December, which it called the most comprehensive in more than 50 years. A public workshop is set for Dec 15 and awards for May or June 2027.

Geothermal developers
Line up matching capital now; the 100% match is the gate.
Drillers and service firms
Comment on the well rule overhaul in December; it sets technical terms for decades.

Sources: California Energy Commission, Nov 23, 2026

Markets

Canada opens a carbon removal buying program to 19 offers, but nothing is bought yet

Canada's Treasury Board announced a national master standing offer on Nov 19 with 19 offers from 17 suppliers across five categories: direct air capture with storage (4), BECCS (3), biochar (5), other biomass removal (4) and enhanced mineralization (3), The Deep Dive reported. A standing offer is not a contract; federal bodies must issue call ups before purchases bind. Ottawa has pledged at least C$10 million of removal purchases by 2030, about $7 million, Argus reported. Remova Carbon Systems of Calgary holds half the potential BECCS dollars for a plant near Whitecourt, Alberta, targeting about 20,000 tonnes a year from 2028. The offers run to March 31, 2029, with an option to 2031.

Removal suppliers
A place on the standing offer is a sales channel, not revenue. Target departments with fleet emissions.
Buyers
Government category splits are a useful benchmark for portfolio mixes.

Sources: The Deep Dive, Nov 19, 2026; Argus Media, Nov 11, 2026

Regulation

Negotiators say Saudi Arabia led resistance to fossil fuel wording at COP31

Negotiators from Europe, small island states, Latin America, Asia and Africa told Reuters that Saudi Arabia was at the forefront of resisting language on reducing reliance on fossil fuels at COP31, the main reason no political agreement was reached, Dawn reported Nov 24. About 130 countries had backed a proposal that any deal mention transitioning away from fossil fuels and drop the reference to carbon capture, Reuters and Carbon Herald reported. The failed text had set at least 500 million tonnes a year of capture capacity by 2035. The Turkish presidency said no group or country received preferential treatment; Saudi representatives did not immediately comment.

CCS developers
Capture is now politically coded in UN talks. Expect buyers and lenders to ask how projects relate to fossil output.
Policy teams
Bonn in June 2027 is the next chance to revisit the wording.

Sources: Reuters via Dawn, Nov 24, 2026; Carbon Herald, Nov 23, 2026

NdRare earths

Vietnam breaks ground on a $60 million rare earth oxide plant in Hai Phong

Khanh An Industrial Park JSC began building a 14 hectare plant with five hydrometallurgical separation lines and 10,000 tonnes a year of capacity for individual oxides above 99% purity, TNGlobal reported Nov 23. Prime Minister Le Minh Hung attended; commercial operation is set from Q4 2028.

Sources: TNGlobal, Nov 23, 2026

Thailand to block rare earth mining chemicals to Myanmar; USA Rare Earth flags Brazil law

Thailand will stop exporting chemicals used in rare earth mining to Myanmar over river pollution, Reuters reported Nov 23. Separately, USA Rare Earth told the SEC that Brazil's Law 15,506/2026, which lets future rules set export conditions and screen foreign control, could affect its Serra Verde mine; no restriction is in place yet, The Rio Times reported.

Sources: Reuters via Mining.com.au, Nov 23, 2026; The Rio Times, Nov 23, 2026

UUranium

Cyberattack disrupts Peninsula Energy's Lance uranium mine in Wyoming

Peninsula said a Nov 20 cyberattack hit Lance's operating server and requested an ASX trading halt; corporate systems were unaffected and it has not said whether production stopped, The Northern Miner reported Nov 23. It had already withdrawn its 2026 target of 400,000 to 500,000 lb U3O8 because of low well flows.

Sources: The Northern Miner, Nov 23, 2026

LiLithium

Permian produced water becomes a lithium source in Midland, Texas

Element3's plant in Midland started up in February 2026 and makes battery grade lithium carbonate from oilfield produced water using technology licensed from Oak Ridge National Laboratory, Industrial Info reported. It is not yet at capacity. The US produces under 1% of world lithium, per USGS.

Sources: Industrial Info Resources, Oct 19, 2026

Worth reading

Assessing the carbon emissions and energy consumption of US hyperscale data centers

Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health, Nature Sustainability, Nov 16, 2026

Satellite imagery found 403 hyperscale sites using 68 to 99 TWh from May 2024 to April 2025, about 1.8% of US electricity and 25.7 Mt of CO2; about 54% of the power was fossil generated.

Ocean takes a growing share of carbon emissions while land absorption stalls

Scripps Institution of Oceanography, UC San Diego, Science, Nov 12, 2026

Thirty years of O2 and CO2 records show the ocean took up 2.8 billion tonnes of CO2 a year from 1990 to 2019, about 35% of fossil emissions, while the land sink peaked around 2009 and has generally declined.
