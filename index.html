COP31 and carbon capture

COP31's draft deal asks the world to build 500 million tonnes of carbon capture by 2035

The first draft cover decision in Antalya puts a number on carbon capture for the first time, alongside an electrification goal. The world runs about 68 million tonnes today, and the text still has to survive a divided room before talks close on Friday.

The COP31 presidency published an 18-page draft cover decision on November 16. It calls on countries to help scale carbon capture, utilisation and storage to at least 500 million tonnes of CO2 a year by 2035 under current policies, with a more ambitious range of 1.3 to 2.5 billion tonnes. It also recognizes carbon dioxide removal as necessary to bring warming back below 1.5°C, as a complement to emissions cuts rather than a substitute. The presidency says the draft does not reflect consensus.

The gap is large. The Global CCS Institute counted 68 million tonnes a year of operating capture capacity and 56 million under construction as of July 31, 2026. Even with every project under construction finished, the 500 million tonne floor is about four times that. The draft also proposes raising electricity from about 20% to 35% of final energy use by 2035 and asks developed countries to phase out unilateral climate trade measures by the end of 2027.

The room is split. EU climate commissioner Wopke Hoekstra said on November 17 the text needs significant change, while the Arab Group and the Like-Minded Developing Countries oppose new sectoral targets such as the electrification pledge. Talks are scheduled to close on November 20, and Australia's Chris Bowen, who leads the negotiations, expects at least one more draft. In Europe's carbon market the mood was soft: the benchmark EU allowance fell more than 5% to €82.95 a tonne on November 18 amid reform talks and weaker equities.

Sources: Carbon Herald, Nov 16, 2026; Carbon Brief, Nov 16, 2026; UNFCCC draft cover decision; Al Jazeera (AFP), Nov 17, 2026; Rio Times, Nov 18, 2026; CO2CRC (Global Status of CCS 2026); Investing.com, Nov 18, 2026; EC Joint Research Centre

Through the nexus

Electrons

The draft's electrification goal, about 35% of final energy from electricity by 2035, would pull more load onto grids that are already stretched by data centers. Capture plants also need power to run, which adds to that demand.

Molecules

The text reaffirms the transition away from fossil fuels and asks for faster cuts in methane from fugitive sources. A capture target gives gas processors, ammonia, cement and steel producers a route to keep using molecules with lower emissions.

Pore space

Capture only counts if the CO2 has somewhere to go. Reaching 500 million tonnes means permitted storage on the same scale, and today's operating capacity of 68 million tonnes shows how far storage permitting and site appraisal must move in nine years.

Water

Capture is thirsty. A European Commission JRC assessment found that adding CO2 capture raises a power plant's water use per unit of output by 50% to 62% for coal and gas combined cycle plants. A sevenfold scale-up of capture is also a large new water demand, often in dry regions.

Use it

Investors
A COP target is a signal, not a subsidy. Value capture projects on national support such as 45Q, the EU ETS and Canada's tax credits, and treat the 500 million tonne figure as upside.
Regulators
If the target survives, storage permitting becomes the bottleneck. Check how many wells your agency can review a year against your country's share of 500 million tonnes.
Industry
Hard to abate sites without a storage plan should start appraisal now. Storage takes years to permit, longer than the capture plant takes to build.
Founders
Lower water and energy use per tonne is a selling point. Tools that cut the water footprint of capture fit a market that may need to grow several times over.

The brief

Capital

North Carolina makes big data centers sign long contracts and pay for their own grid

The North Carolina Utilities Commission on November 16 approved Duke Energy's first separate rate schedule for data centers of 50 MW or more. They must pay demand charges on at least 75% of contracted load, sign for 10 years, or 15 years at 100 MW and above, pay upfront for dedicated infrastructure, post guarantees for transmission upgrades and pay exit fees if they leave early. Data centers are 92% of the 6.7 GW of new contracted demand Duke signed from March 2024 to April 2026. The same day the commission approved residential increases of about 9.5% and 9.3% over two years and a plan with nearly 3,850 MW of new gas generation. In Kentucky, Kentucky Power asked regulators on November 18 to approve a $2.81 billion, 769 MW gas plant to serve a planned 1 GW TeraWulf data center from 2031.

Investors
Large load tariffs shift stranded asset risk from households to tech tenants. Read the minimum take and exit terms before valuing utility capex plans.
Developers
Expect 10 to 15 year commitments and collateral as the price of grid access in more US states.

Sources: WRAL, Nov 17, 2026; WCLU (Kentucky Power), Nov 18, 2026

Projects

India gets a $3.6 billion green ammonia plant with buyers in hand

ACME Group took a final investment decision on November 18 on a $3.6 billion green ammonia plant in Odisha, designed for 760,000 tonnes a year and commissioning by 2029. About 60% of output is contracted: 370,000 tonnes a year to Indian fertilizer makers through SECI's SIGHT scheme and 83,000 tonnes to Japan's IHI under Japan's contract for difference program. Thyssenkrupp Uhde supplies the ammonia loop, and Brookfield has committed $600 million to ACME's green molecules business. In Spain, Repsol approved a 150 MW electrolyzer at Tarragona, about €400 million with up to €104 million in public support, to feed a renewable methanol plant.

Investors
Signed offtake is what moved this project to FID. Use contracted share of output as the first screen for hydrogen deals.
Buyers
The plant is designed to meet EU RFNBO rules, so European buyers can bid for the uncontracted 40%.

Sources: Business Standard, Nov 18, 2026; Rigzone, Nov 15, 2026

Regulation

Colorado becomes the seventh state to permit its own CO2 storage wells

Colorado's Energy and Carbon Management Commission took over Class VI permitting and enforcement on November 12, after EPA published its final rule on October 13. It joins Arizona, Louisiana, North Dakota, Texas, West Virginia and Wyoming. Carbon America's Denova project, which would store emissions from Sterling Ethanol and Yuma Ethanol, is under state review. A state official estimated fewer than a dozen companies may pursue new storage projects in Colorado in the next year or two.

Developers
State primacy usually means a single regulator for wells and surface rules. Ask the commission for its review timeline before you file.
Landowners
Pore space and long term liability rules differ by state. Read Colorado's before signing a storage lease.

Sources: Carbon Herald; KUNC, Oct 29, 2026; Colorado ECMC

Water

AI's biggest water draw sits at the power plant, not the server hall

Using Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory figures, Forbes reported on October 30 that US data centers used about 17.4 billion gallons for cooling in 2023, while the power plants supplying them used about 211 billion, roughly 12 gallons at the plant for each gallon on site. Federal lab data put gas plants near 198 gallons per MWh, coal and nuclear near 690 and 670, and wind close to zero. Local data are mixed: in Durham, North Carolina, four likely data centers used 0.2% of city water, WRAL reported on November 16, yet Raleigh and Durham have paused some new data center approvals. Google signed on November 17 for 50 MW of superhot geothermal power from Quaise in Central Oregon for its data centers in The Dalles, from as early as 2030.

Regulators
Cooling rules miss most of the footprint. Count the water used by the generation a data center contracts, not just the site.
Operators
Low water power such as wind, solar and geothermal can ease local permitting fights over new load.

Sources: Forbes, Oct 30, 2026; WRAL, Nov 16, 2026; Quaise Energy, Nov 17, 2026

Projects

Europe's first full cross-border CO2 chain is running, but the storage pipeline is thin

The European Commission said on November 17 that Yara's Sluiskil ammonia plant in the Netherlands began capturing CO2 in September, up to 800,000 tonnes a year shipped to Northern Lights under the Norwegian North Sea. Porthos in the Netherlands and Greensand in Denmark are still under construction. The EU's Net-Zero Industry Act targets at least 50 million tonnes of annual storage capacity by 2030, and the Commission plans legislation for an internal CO2 market in early 2027. Northern Lights now has seven industrial customers in four countries and is expanding from 1.5 to more than 5 million tonnes a year.

Industry
Storage slots are scarce before 2030. Emitters planning capture should secure transport and storage terms early.
Investors
Watch the 2027 CO2 market proposal. Tariff and access rules will set returns for pipelines, ships and terminals.

Sources: European Commission, Nov 17, 2026; Bioenergy International, Sep 2026; Northern Lights, Oct 1, 2026

Critical minerals and mining

The metals behind grids, batteries, magnets and reactors. Supply sits in a few countries, so policy moves prices as much as demand does, and every mine and refinery is also a water and power project.

NdRare earths

Malaysia bends its raw export ban, for France only

On Nov 17 Malaysia granted France a one-off exception to its 2024 ban on exporting raw rare earths, in return for local processing. A 10 year venture between France's Carester and Malaysia's Malaco Mining will run a separation plant in Perak handling about 13,000 tonnes a year from early 2027. In China, praseodymium-neodymium oxide offers kept rising on Nov 18 on tighter supply of cheaper material.

Sources: The Rakyat Post, Nov 18, 2026; SMM, Nov 18, 2026

CuCopper

Funds sell, Chinese buyers step in

LME copper fell as much as 1.4% to about $13,210 a tonne on Nov 16, its lowest since July 8 and about 10% below September's record, on an AI stock selloff, rate hike bets and weak Chinese data. Physical demand held: the Yangshan premium stayed near a record $290 a tonne and Shanghai inventories fell 18% in a week.

Sources: SMM, Nov 17, 2026

LiLithium

Prices at a 2026 low; miners at 12 month lows

Chinese lithium carbonate traded near 90,000 yuan a tonne on Nov 17, a 2026 low. The LIT lithium miners fund and Albemarle both closed at 12 month lows the same day. Lithium hydroxide futures open in Guangzhou on Nov 26.

Sources: Rio Times, Nov 17, 2026

NiNickel

Jakarta hints at a tighter 2027 ore quota

Indonesia's energy minister Bahlil Lahadalia blamed excess output for falling prices and called for supply control through the RKAB quota system, and officials hinted the 2027 ceiling could fall below the revised 2026 level. No figure has been set, and the nickel study group expects a larger surplus next year.

Sources: Energy News, Nov 3, 2026

UUranium

Spot eases, term holds at $99, miners raise cash

TradeTech put spot uranium at $90 a pound in the week to Nov 13, down $2, with the long term price steady at $99. Paladin Energy raised A$525 million at a 10% discount to help fund its Patterson Lake South project in Canada, and its shares fell about 13% in the week.

Sources: FNArena, Nov 17, 2026

§Minerals policy

US defense rules on Chinese rare earths near a deadline

Defense Department sourcing rules that bar Chinese-origin rare earth metals face a January 2027 deadline. China produced about 69% of the world's mined rare earths in 2025, and the Pentagon announced $1.2 billion in loans for domestic projects in June.

Sources: TokenPost, Nov 18, 2026

Oregon State University

A redox-active metal-organic framework called BVR-X keeps water and CO2 in separate pores, capturing CO2 from humid gas with only 4% CO2 over dozens of cycles. Published in Angewandte Chemie.
