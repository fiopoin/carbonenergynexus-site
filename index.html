Climate talks

COP31 ends without a deal, and its carbon capture and electrification goals fall with it

Talks in Antalya closed on Sunday, two days late, with no cover decision after the EU and others objected to a text that named no fossil fuels. A draft goal of 500 million tonnes a year of capture by 2035 and a 35% electrification target went down with it.

COP31 President Murat Kurum told the closing plenary on Sunday, Nov 22 that there was no agreement on the text, UN News and ABC News reported. The EU formally objected, joined by the UK, Norway, Panama, Belize, the Marshall Islands and Vanuatu, among others. Ireland's Darragh O'Brien, speaking for the EU, said it was unconscionable that the text made no reference to fossil fuels. China and India accepted the text, and Ghana, for the African Group, said it would not block it. The last summit to end without an overarching agreement was Copenhagen in 2009, IntelliNews noted.

The failed text carried two numbers this publication's readers track. Its third draft set a target of at least 500 million tonnes a year of industrial carbon capture by 2035, pushed by Saudi Arabia and other oil producers, Reuters reported via IntelliNews. It also aimed for electricity to reach 35% of final energy by 2035, up from about 23% in 2026, while calling for the greatest possible use of clean energy rather than renewables alone, ABC reported. Vanuatu's minister said capture had been singled out for attention it did not deserve.

Some pieces survived. Parties adopted a just transition mechanism, with views due by March 15, 2027, and a finance reporting timetable, The Planet Brief reported. Outside the formal text, at least 75 countries backed a voluntary 35 by 35 electrification pledge, and the presidency listed voluntary agreements on geothermal and greener freight corridors. Unresolved items go to the Bonn talks in June 2027, ahead of COP32 in Addis Ababa from Nov 8 to 19, 2027.

Sources: UN News, Nov 22, 2026; ABC News, Nov 22, 2026; IntelliNews via Yahoo, Nov 23, 2026; The Planet Brief, Nov 22, 2026; Euronews, Nov 22, 2026; MIT Technology Review, Nov 17, 2026

Electrons

Molecules

The final text referred back to the COP28 UAE Consensus but named no fossil fuels. Russia said it could not agree to any fossil fuel wording, Reuters reported.

Pore space

No multilateral capture number exists after Antalya. Storage economics still rest on national credits and rules such as 45Q, Norway's and the EU's frameworks, not a COP target.

Water

The text dropped a $120 billion adaptation finance figure and only agreed to discuss tripling by 2035. Drought is already an energy issue: US Southwest hydropower ran 28% below its 10 year average from April to August, EIA data show.

The brief

Capital

US Senate passes permitting bill 62 to 33; data centers would pay for grid upgrades

The Senate passed S. 5653, the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, on Thursday night, Nov 19, by 62 to 33, NACo and NewsOn6 reported. The bill narrows what triggers NEPA review, sets deadlines for each review step, limits court challenges and bars the federal government from rescinding permits for already authorized projects. Data centers would cover the full cost of the grid upgrades they need, and a new DOE program would advise communities on data center energy and water impacts. Sen. James Lankford put projects waiting on federal permits at about $1 trillion, his estimate. The House returns Nov 30, and its vote before Democrats take both chambers in January is uncertain.

Investors
Permit certainty is the capital story. Watch whether the anti rescission clause survives the House.
Data center developers
Budget for full grid upgrade costs in US sites if this becomes law.

Sources: NACo, Nov 20, 2026; NewsOn6, Nov 20, 2026; Dakota Free Press, Nov 20, 2026

Regulation

Outside the failed text, electrification and geothermal move by voluntary pledge

With no cover decision at COP31, the presidency pointed to voluntary agreements: a 35 by 35 Global Electrification Pledge that the UN said at least 75 countries backed, plus pacts on geothermal by 2050 and greener freight corridors, ABC News and IntelliNews reported. Co-president Chris Bowen of Australia called the overall outcome very disappointing. Parties did adopt a just transition mechanism and a finance reporting timetable, and the Standing Committee on Finance reports on the collective finance goal in November 2027. COP32 is set for Addis Ababa, Nov 8 to 19, 2027.

Policy teams
Voluntary pledges have no compliance path. Track which signatories add them to national plans.
Geothermal developers
A named geothermal pact gives a hook for development bank conversations.

Sources: ABC News, Nov 22, 2026; IntelliNews via Yahoo, Nov 23, 2026; The Planet Brief, Nov 22, 2026

Water

Southwest hydropower ran 28% below average as Lake Powell neared turbine limits

The US Southwest generated 28% less hydropower from April to August than its 10 year average, MIT Technology Review reported on Nov 17, citing EIA data. At the end of September Lake Powell stood at 3,518 feet, less than 1% above the level needed to run water through the Glen Canyon turbines, and Lake Mead was 9% above Hoover Dam's 950 foot minimum. The two plants produced nearly 7 TWh in 2025. EIA projects 2026 hydropower at its lowest in more than a decade, falling further in 2027. October and early November floods lifted Lake Powell more than 20 feet from its September low.

Utilities
Plan for hydro shortfalls with firm replacement capacity, which in the West often means gas or storage.
Investors
Storage and transmission into the Southwest gain value as hydro becomes less reliable.

Sources: MIT Technology Review, Nov 17, 2026; US EIA, Today in Energy

Projects

Dow's four reactor Texas nuclear plant holds a permit but not yet an FID

The NRC approved a construction permit on Nov 9 for the Long Mott Generating Station at Dow's Seadrift site in Texas: four X-energy Xe-100 reactors of 80 MWe or 200 MWt each, up to 320 MWe, supplying power and high temperature steam, DOE said Nov 13. It is the first construction permit for a high temperature gas cooled reactor in more than 50 years and the first advanced nuclear plant approved at a US industrial site. Dow still has to take a final investment decision; construction could begin before 2030, Inside Climate News reported Nov 22, and DOE targets first energy in the early 2030s.

Industry
Process steam, not just power, is the case here. Heat users should watch Dow's FID.
Investors
A permit removes licensing risk for construction only; an operating licence comes later.

Sources: US DOE Office of Nuclear Energy, Nov 13, 2026; Inside Climate News via ZME Science, Nov 22, 2026

Critical minerals and mining

Copper opened the week below $12,000 a ton, while policy deadlines set the pace elsewhere: China's pause on its gallium, germanium and antimony export ban to the US ends Nov 27, and Indonesia plans a smaller 2027 nickel ore quota.

NdRare earths

Myanmar's rare earth mining sites multiply as buyers circle

Kachin State mining sites have tripled since the 2021 coup, and UN figures cited by Eurasia Review show Shan State sites rising from 12 to 85. Myanmar's rare earth exports to China from 2017 to 2024 topped $4 billion, 84% of it after the coup, the Nov 22 analysis said, noting that China does about 90% of world refining and that the US, India and Russia have all sought access.

Sources: Eurasia Review, Nov 22, 2026

DyHeavy rare earths

Canadian rare earth developers still face funding and permitting gaps

Ucore Rare Metals, owner of the Bokan Dotson Ridge heavy rare earth project in Alaska, is valued at about C$213 million and is pre revenue; Mkango, whose Songwe Hill project in Malawi targets Nd, Pr, Dy and Tb, at about C$253 million; and Aclara at about C$504 million, JournalArta reported Nov 22. Financing and permits remain unresolved for all three.

Sources: JournalArta, Nov 22, 2026

CuCopper

LME copper dips below $12,000 a ton after its worst week in 19 months

Three month LME copper fell to about $11,959 a ton early Monday, its weakest since March, then recovered to $12,240.50 by 03:40 GMT, Investing.com reported. It lost about 9% last week on doubts over AI data center spending, a stronger dollar and rate expectations. The record was near $15,000 in September, Business Upturn noted, and uncertainty over US tariffs on refined copper persists.

Sources: Investing.com, Nov 22, 2026; Business Upturn, Nov 23, 2026

§Gallium, germanium, antimony

China's pause on its US export ban for gallium and germanium ends Nov 27

China's one year suspension of the ban on shipping gallium, germanium, antimony and superhard materials to the US closes Nov 27, 2026, with no public sign yet of an extension, Startup Fortune reported. The ban on dual use exports to US military users stays in force, and the licensing regimes for gallium and germanium from 2023 and antimony from 2024 remain.

Sources: Startup Fortune, Oct 31, 2026

NiNickel

Indonesia signals a smaller 2027 nickel ore quota

Indonesia's mines ministry said the 2027 nickel ore quota will be lower than the revised 2026 quota of 290 to 300 million tonnes, citing smelter cuts and a global surplus, Mysteel reported Oct 30. SMM put the surplus near 800,000 tonnes on Nov 4 and said 2027 cuts may be more aggressive than this year's. No figure has been published.

Sources: Mysteel, Oct 30, 2026; SMM, Nov 4, 2026

UUranium

Uranium futures hold near nine month highs

US uranium futures traded above $91 a pound in November, the highest since February, Trading Economics reported Nov 10, as Kazatomprom's September quarter sales rose 25% and the IAEA counted 78 reactors under construction. Cameco's long term price indicator stood at $98 a pound at the end of October, InvestorNews reported.

Sources: Trading Economics, Nov 10, 2026; InvestorNews, Nov 8, 2026
