Carbon markets and policy

Europe hands heavy industry more free carbon permits as COP31 enters its final week

Brussels agreed to soften carbon costs for energy-intensive plants through 2030. The price signal that capture, storage and removal projects lean on just got weaker, and taxpayers may be asked to fill the gap.

On November 12, EU governments and the European Parliament reached a provisional deal to raise free carbon allowances for industrial installations whose allocation is set by the ETS heat and fuel benchmarks, covering 2026 to 2030. The deal provides a cushion of roughly 121 million free allowances for sectors judged at risk of carbon leakage, above the Commission's original proposal of about 88 million.

The market had been firm going in. The December 2026 EU allowance contract closed at €90.5 a tonne on November 2, its highest since January. The longer view is softer: IEEFA reported on November 11 that ABN AMRO cut its 2030 EU carbon price forecast by 37% in under a year, and argued that a weaker carbon price shifts the cost of industrial decarbonization from polluters to public budgets.

Negotiations are not finished. Ireland, which chairs the Council, circulated a compromise that would slow the phase-out of free permits and exempt smaller installations from new decarbonization requirements. The Commission's July 17 ETS review also proposes bringing 250 million tonnes of permanent domestic removals, from bioenergy with CCS and direct air capture, into the system between 2031 and 2040.

Sources: Brussels Times, Nov 13, 2026; Eunews, Nov 13, 2026; GMK Center (ICE data), Nov 2026; IEEFA, Nov 11, 2026; Global Banking & Finance Review (Reuters), Nov 15, 2026; MLex, Nov 5, 2026; A&O Shearman, ETS revision; EC Joint Research Centre; IEAGHG 2020-09

Through the nexus

Electrons

Power generators are not the target of this deal. It covers industrial installations whose free allowances depend on heat and fuel use, so electricity's carbon cost stays tied to the market price.

Molecules

Plants that burn fuel for process heat, such as paper and other energy-intensive producers, get lower compliance bills through 2030. Less cost pressure means less urgency to switch fuels or electrify heat.

Pore space

Capture and storage projects compete with the price of an allowance. A lower or less certain price weakens the business case, while the proposed 250 million tonne removals channel after 2031 is the clearest long-run demand signal for European storage.

Water

Carbon capture is thirsty. A European Commission JRC assessment found that adding CO2 capture raises a plant's water use per unit of power by 50% to 62% for coal and gas combined cycle plants. Storage has its own water story: pulling brine out of a storage formation frees space for CO2, turning a disposal cost into a treatment opportunity.

Use it

Investors
Treat EU carbon price forecasts as policy exposed. Run any CCS, removals or industrial decarbonization model at a lower allowance price as well as the current one.
Regulators
Watch whether the final text ties free allowances to real decarbonization investment. A November 5 draft reported by MLex would let companies keep most of them even without it.
Industry
If your site is allocated on heat or fuel benchmarks, your 2026 to 2030 free allocation is likely to rise. Confirm with your national authority once the text is final.
Founders
Removal developers should read the 250 million tonne purchasing proposal closely. A compliance buyer is worth more than any voluntary offtake.

The brief

Regulation

A year after Texas took over carbon storage permits, the queue is the story

EPA approved the Railroad Commission of Texas as the Class VI permitting authority on November 12, 2025, making Texas the sixth state with primacy. At the time, more than 60 Class VI applications were pending in Texas, about 24% of EPA's national backlog. Louisiana, the first large primacy state, had issued one permit since 2023 and its governor paused new reviews in October 2025. Carbon Pulse has since reported that new Texas applications have slowed and that long-term liability is still unsettled.

Investors
Permit timing is now a state by state variable. Price Gulf Coast storage on Texas timelines, not EPA's.
Landowners
Pore space leases are long contracts. Ask who holds liability after injection stops before you sign.

Sources: Clifford Chance, Jan 12, 2026; Gravel2Gavel (Pillsbury); CRS R48033, Oct 19, 2026; Carbon Pulse

Capital

45Q survived the tax bill. The fight now is over who checks the tonnes

The 45Q credit pays $85 per tonne of CO2 captured at a facility and $180 for direct air capture, and the 2025 tax law set equal values across storage and use. Treasury's Notice 2026-50, issued August 14, extended the safe harbor that lets companies report stored volumes while EPA's reporting rules are in flux. IEEFA argued on October 22 that the change makes claims harder to verify. GAO counted 33 US carbon capture facilities as of March 2026 and found 45Q claims more than tripled from 2019 to 2023.

Investors
The credit value is intact. The risk to model is verification and recapture, not repeal.
Operators
PwC advises checking you can meet the independent certification requirement by the time you file.

Sources: IEEFA, Oct 22, 2026; Holland & Knight, Sep 2026; GAO-26-107711, Aug 6, 2026; PwC, Sep 10, 2026

Projects

Canada's biggest carbon capture bet hit its signing deadline

Ottawa, Alberta and the five Oil Sands Alliance producers set November 15 as the target for binding agreements on the Pathways CCS project, which aims to capture about 6 million tonnes a year by 2035 and 16 million by 2045. Ottawa has committed to extend investment tax credits of 50% on capture equipment and 37.5% on transport and storage. Alberta unveiled lower royalties on some new oil sands output on November 13. A Cenovus executive called the timeline tight on November 4. We found no public confirmation of signed agreements as of this morning. The alliance targets a final investment decision in late 2027 or early 2028.

Investors
Canadian Natural says its 2027 spending plan depends on these agreements. Expect producer guidance to move with them.
Suppliers
Pre-FID engineering for the capture sites and the shared CO2 pipeline is the contract flow to watch in 2027.

Sources: PR Newswire, Sep 2026; CP24, Jul 13, 2026; Globe and Mail, Nov 13, 2026; CP24, Nov 4, 2026; The Canadian Press; National Observer, Jul 13, 2026; Decarbonfuse

Markets

At COP31, governments start acting like carbon removal buyers

COP31 runs in Antalya through November 20, with Türkiye holding the presidency and Australia leading negotiations. On November 11, fifteen countries released a statement on policies to scale carbon credit markets, while delegates split over when credits should be authorized under Article 6. The same day, a Canadian official said the government would announce competitive standing offers for carbon removal in the coming days, part of a plan to buy at least C$10 million of durable removals from direct air capture, bioenergy with CCS and enhanced mineralization.

Founders
Government procurement is small in dollars and large in signal. A standing offer is a reference price you can show lenders.
Buyers
Article 6 authorization timing decides whether a credit can count toward a country's target. Ask sellers which status yours carries.

Sources: Sylvera COP31; Carbon Pulse, Nov 11, 2026; Carbon Pulse ticker, Nov 11, 2026; Argus, Nov 11, 2026

Water

The carbon business is becoming a water business

Capture6 opened Project Tortuga near Perth on October 12: eight containerised units that recover freshwater from brine and capture CO2 from the air, funded under Australia's Carbon Capture Technologies Program. The company estimates that treating Australia's brine could yield up to 470 gigalitres of extra freshwater a year. It is also building Project Monarch for California's Palmdale Water District, due to start operating in the second quarter of 2027. Separately, a KAUST led team reported in Nature that recirculating groundwater lets CO2 be turned to stone in basalt without the large freshwater volumes once assumed; 70% of 131 tons injected in western Saudi Arabia mineralized within ten months.

Investors
Projects that sell both water and carbon removal have two revenue lines. Ask which one carries the economics.
Operators
Brine you pay to dispose of is feedstock for these models. Produced water and storage brine are the supply to watch.

Sources: Water Online, Oct 13, 2026; EurekAlert (KAUST), Mar 25, 2026

Projects

Northern Lights moves its second phase into fabrication

Worley Rosenberg won a Subsea7 contract on October 16 to fabricate subsea structures and spools for Northern Lights Phase II, which expands Norway's open-access CO2 transport and storage network to more than 5 million tonnes a year by 2028. Phase II is the clearest test of whether third-party storage can be sold like pipeline capacity to emitters across Europe.

Industry
European emitters without their own storage can plan around shipped CO2 to Norway on a 2028 horizon.
Investors
Open-access storage tariffs are the number to track. They set the floor cost of a stored tonne in Europe.

Sources: World Oil, Oct 16, 2026
