Storage safety

Study links two felt Edmonton earthquakes to CO2 injection at Quest, a first for carbon storage

University of Bristol researchers say the ML 4.0 and 4.2 quakes in April and May were likely induced by a decade of injection at Shell's Quest project, and that standard forecasting models would have seen them coming.

Two earthquakes widely felt across Edmonton on April 1 and May 9, 2026, measured ML 4.0 and ML 4.2, were likely caused by CO2 injection at the Quest carbon capture and storage project, according to a study by James Verdon, Cindy Lim and Germán Rodríguez-Pradilla of the University of Bristol in the International Journal of Greenhouse Gas Control (October 2026). The authors call it the first known example of felt induced seismicity caused by CCS operations. Phys.org coverage on Nov 27 brought the paper wide attention. The Alberta Energy Regulator said it received no reports of damage or injuries after the May event.

Quest captures CO2 from hydrogen production at the Scotford Upgrader north of Edmonton and has stored more than 10 million tonnes since 2015, about 1 million tonnes a year, through three wells into the Basal Cambrian Sandstone saline aquifer about 2 km down. The quakes sat deeper, at about 3.6 km and 4.6 km in the upper Precambrian basement, within the expected pressure footprint. The paper rules out the nearby Redwater waterflood oilfield as a cause, and says a seismogenic index model and an extreme value model would both have forecast ML 4 events before they happened.

The paper lists mitigation options used elsewhere: lower injection rates, regular pauses, injecting into layers hydraulically isolated from the basement and, in extreme cases, producing water out of the formation to relieve pressure. It also asks for history matched reservoir models, better seismic coverage and a fuller microseismic catalogue. The findings land as Alberta expands storage hubs and as the IEA pathway calls for billions of tonnes of storage a year by 2050.

Sources: Verdon, Lim and Rodríguez-Pradilla, Int. J. Greenhouse Gas Control, Oct 2026; The Morning Science via Phys.org, Nov 27, 2026; News Minimalist, Nov 27, 2026

Through the nexus

Electrons

Quest's CO2 comes from hydrogen made at Scotford. Any injection limits would ripple back to the low carbon hydrogen and fuels it supports.

Molecules

More than 10 million tonnes of CO2 stored since 2015 at about 1 million tonnes a year, the paper says, making Quest one of the longest running dedicated storage sites.

Pore space

Pressure from injection 2 km down reached faults in the basement below. Seal and caprock are not the only risk; what sits beneath the reservoir matters too.

Water

The storage zone is a deep saline aquifer, and one mitigation the authors name is pulling brine back out to lower pressure, which creates a produced water stream to handle.

Use it

Investors
Seismic monitoring and rate limits are now a real operating risk for storage hubs. Ask how often injection could be curtailed.
Regulators
The paper says common forecasting models would have flagged ML 4 events. Traffic light rules can be set before injection scales.
Industry
Map basement faults and plan for pressure management, including possible brine production, from the permit stage.
Founders
Better microseismic catalogues and pressure forecasting are gaps the authors name directly. That is a market.

Capital

CF Industries buys Mosaic's Louisiana ammonia plant for $600 million and will study CO2 capture there

CF Industries agreed to buy Mosaic's Faustina ammonia complex in St. James, Louisiana, for a total of $600 million: $360 million in cash and $240 million tied to a 20 year deal to supply Mosaic up to 200,000 tons of ammonia a year, Carbon Herald reported Nov 27. The plant has run at about 60% utilization over five years against CF's 96% network average. CF plans about $100 million over three years to lift output from about 300,000 tons a year to 480,000 in year one and about 570,000 by 2030, and will evaluate CO2 dehydration and compression for low carbon ammonia with geological storage. Closing is targeted for the first half of 2027.

Investors
Brownfield ammonia plus a CCS option is a cheaper route than new build. Watch the capture decision.
Storage developers
Another Gulf Coast ammonia source may need pipeline and pore space. Position early.

Sources: Carbon Herald, Nov 27, 2026

Regulation

EU sets the CBAM timetable: certificate sales open Feb 1, 2027, first surrender due Sept 30

Under Commission document C(2026)8367, importers can buy CBAM certificates from Feb 1, 2027 and must cover 2026 imports by Sept 30, 2027, when the first annual declaration and surrender are due, Carbon Herald reported Nov 27. From 2027, importers must hold certificates for at least 50% of embedded emissions at each quarter end. One certificate equals one tonne of CO2e, adjusted for free allocation and carbon prices paid abroad. Prices are set quarterly for 2026 imports and weekly from 2027. The Q3 2026 CBAM price was fixed at €82.32, and the December EUA contract stood at €83.75 on Nov 23, CBAM News reported.

Exporters to the EU
Document carbon prices you already pay. They reduce certificates owed.
Importers
Budget for 2026 imports now and plan quarterly holdings from 2027.

Sources: Carbon Herald, Nov 27, 2026; CBAM News, Nov 24, 2026

Water

Canadian lithium brine moves: E3 starts Phase 3 at Clearwater, Prairie Lithium pulls first brine

E3 Lithium advanced its Clearwater project near Olds, Alberta to Phase 3 of its demonstration program, backed by up to C$36.5 million from Natural Resources Canada, after its producer and injector well pair produced more than 80,000 cubic metres of Leduc brine, Wisevoter reported Nov 27, citing Mining Weekly. The Alberta Energy Regulator moved its processing facility application to the second and final phase. In Saskatchewan, Prairie Lithium produced its first surface brine from a Duperow Formation well at Pad #1, feeding a four column direct lithium extraction unit, Mining Technology reported Nov 23. It has C$6.3 million in interest free federal financing.

Water managers
DLE projects produce and reinject large brine volumes. Track disposal and injection well permits.
Oil and gas operators
Old wells and brine know how are transferring to lithium. Look for partnership demand.

Sources: Wisevoter via Mining Weekly, Nov 27, 2026; Mining Technology, Nov 23, 2026

Projects

Spain funds 552 MWh of hybrid batteries and a German investor enters the Dutch battery market

Spain's MITECO awarded €56.1 million to three Iberdrola battery projects in Granada province, each 44.5 MW and 183.94 MWh, co-located with solar and wind, ESS News reported Nov 27. The programme now funds 130 projects with €826 million, about 2.2 GW and 9.4 GWh, due by end 2029. In the Netherlands, Hamburg based Blue Elephant Energy bought 95% of the 40 MW, 160 MWh Zuidvelde battery from TPSolar; it shares the solar park's grid connection, is financed by ING and should be commissioned in Q3 2027, Energy Global reported Nov 27.

Developers
Co-location and cable pooling avoid new grid connections. Use them where queues are long.
Lenders
Public grants are filling hybrid battery stacks in Spain. Model them into debt sizing.

Sources: ESS News, Nov 27, 2026; Energy Global, Nov 27, 2026

Markets

EU carbon recovers to €83.75 after its sharpest weekly drop in nine months

The December 2026 EUA contract fell 7.8% in the week to Nov 20 to close at €81.26, touching €80.03, the lowest since late July, on volume of 183 million allowances, CBAM News reported. It rebounded 3.06% to €83.75 on Nov 23. The market traded above €90 as recently as mid November. The provisional Q4 CBAM price was €86.69, with the final figure due in early January after the last 2026 primary auction on Dec 14.

Compliance buyers
Volatility is up ahead of the Parliament vote. Stagger purchases.
Analysts
The EUA now feeds CBAM prices directly. Track both together.

Sources: CBAM News, Nov 24, 2026; CBAM News, Nov 24, 2026

Critical minerals and mining

NdRare earths

Lynas tells its AGM of a 5,000 t NdPr offtake with a $110 a kg floor to 2038

Lynas reported FY26 revenue of A$977.9 million and 7,260 tonnes of NdPr at its Nov 25 AGM, and cited an amended agreement with Japan's JARE carrying a 5,000 tonne firm NdPr offtake at a floor of US$110 a kg, in force to 2038, Kalkine reported. Its Malaysian licence was renewed for 10 years, and a JS Link deal targets a 3,000 tonne NdFeB magnet plant in Kuantan. In China, SMM said Pr-Nd oxide quotes edged down on Nov 27 on weak demand.

Sources: Kalkine, Nov 2026; SMM, Nov 27, 2026

USA Rare Earth traded at $6.52 on Nov 27, down 27% over a month, after Canaccord cut its target 69% and kept a Buy rating, 24/7 Wall St reported. Q3 revenue was $3 million against $22.9 million cost of revenue. The company guides at least 325 tonnes of TREO sold from Serra Verde in Q4 and 4,000 tonnes a year after Stage 1 in Q2 2027.

NiNickel

Indonesian nickel faces a sulphur squeeze that puts 600 kt of new capacity at risk

Indonesia's sulphur imports fell 29% in the first half of 2026 after the Strait of Hormuz closure and average prices nearly tripled, putting more than 600 kt of incoming MHP nickel capacity at risk, Wood Mackenzie said in a Nov 27 summary. It also expects global refined cobalt use to contract in 2026. GEM shipped 30,812 tonnes of contained nickel from Indonesia in Q3, SMM reported Nov 27.

Sources: Batteries News, Nov 27, 2026; SMM, Nov 27, 2026

LiLithium

China lithium carbonate drops to CNY 84,350 a tonne, down almost 16% in a month

Battery grade lithium carbonate fell 2.03% on Nov 27 to CNY 84,350 a tonne, down 15.69% over a month and 10.03% over a year, Trading Economics data show. In Chile, the water agency is collecting community input on a water plan for the Salar de Atacama, where SQM reported net brine extraction of 1,017 litres a second in 2025, Discovery Alert reported Nov 16.

Sources: Trading Economics, Nov 27, 2026; Discovery Alert, Nov 16, 2026

Uranium holds near $90 a pound as Energy Fuels plans a 1 million lb milling run
