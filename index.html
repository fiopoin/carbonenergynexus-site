Carbon removal

IEA raises its 2050 carbon removal need by 30% as operating capture capacity sits at 68 Mt a year

The IEA's net zero pathway now needs 6.4 billion tonnes of CO2 captured a year by 2050. UK cement and lime makers say they need firm support to build their share.

The IEA's World Energy Outlook 2026 net zero scenario needs about 2.7 billion tonnes a year of removals from direct air capture and bioenergy with storage by 2050, about 30% more than its previous outlook, Carbon Herald reported Nov 25. The IEA links the increase to higher emissions in 2025 and a resulting temperature overshoot. Total CO2 capture rises from about 100 million tonnes in 2025 to 2.2 billion tonnes in 2035, 3.9 billion in 2040 and 6.4 billion in 2050. The Global CCS Institute counts 113 commercial projects operating with 68 million tonnes a year of capture capacity, and cautions that capacity is not the same as CO2 actually stored.

Industry accounts for more than a quarter of captured emissions by 2035 in the IEA pathway, with cement, steel and chemicals named. In Westminster on Nov 25, Breedon, Tarmac, Holcim, Buxton Lime, the GMB, Unite and the TUC signed a Cement and Lime Charter asking the UK government for long term certainty on carbon capture support, Carbon Herald reported Nov 26. Their Peak Cluster project would link four Derbyshire and Staffordshire plants making about 40% of UK cement and lime, capture more than 3 million tonnes a year, and pipe it to the Morecambe Net Zero store under the East Irish Sea. A development consent application is planned for 2027.

New capture and removal plants will compete for power and water with other new loads. In Nevada, a Desert Research Institute report estimates data centers could use 13,000 acre feet of water a year directly by 2036, about 4 billion gallons, plus 22,000 acre feet indirectly, and an amount of power equal to all of Nevada's 2024 generation, Planetizen reported Nov 24, citing Facilities Dive.

Sources: Carbon Herald, Nov 25, 2026; Carbon Herald, Nov 26, 2026; Planetizen via Facilities Dive, Nov 24, 2026; IEA World Energy Outlook 2026

Through the nexus

Electrons

In the IEA net zero case, electricity rises from under a quarter of final energy today to 35% by 2035, with low emissions sources near 90% of generation.

Molecules

Cement's CO2 comes mostly from limestone itself, not fuel, which is why the industry says capture is its main route. Industry is over a quarter of IEA captured volumes by 2035.

Pore space

6.4 billion tonnes a year by 2050 means storage on a scale far beyond the 68 Mt a year of capture capacity running today. Peak Cluster relies on one offshore store, Morecambe Net Zero.

Water

Capture and removal sites draw on the same water as data centers. Nevada's estimate of 13,000 acre feet a year of direct data center use by 2036 shows how fast that demand can grow.

The brief

Capital

Australia sets a record for renewable investment decisions as big battery commitments double

New large scale renewable capacity reaching final investment decision hit 2.1 GW in the September quarter, taking 2026 to 5 GW, above all of 2025, and a record 5.5 GW including deals after the quarter, the Clean Energy Regulator's quarterly report shows, RenewEconomy reported Nov 26. Wind took 1.1 GW, its strongest quarter on record. Big batteries reached 13.8 GWh of FIDs in the quarter and 20 GWh for the year, and the average new battery grew to 621 MWh from 265 MWh in 2025. NEM wholesale prices fell 27% from a year earlier.

Investors
More than half of FIDs had Capacity Investment Scheme backing. Watch the next tender rounds.
Storage developers
Bigger batteries are now the norm. Plan grid connection for 600 MWh plus systems.

Sources: RenewEconomy, Nov 26, 2026; Energy-Storage.news, Nov 2026

Projects

China commissions a 195 km pipeline built only for hydrogen in Inner Mongolia

The pipeline links green hydrogen production in Darhan Muminggan United Banner with industrial users and is designed for up to 100,000 tonnes a year, BioEnergy Times reported Nov 26, citing Xinhua. Transport cost is expected to fall about 77% at full capacity, to about 3 yuan a kg from 13. Developer Inner Mongolia Huadian Hydrogen Energy said it will inform larger networks, and Baotou Steel made steel for hydrogen embrittlement. In the same week, China Energy Engineering's Songyuan park got its first central incentive funds and its Phase I produces over 700 tonnes of green ammonia a day, SMM reported.

Hydrogen developers
Dedicated pipe changes delivered cost more than electrolyser price. Compare trucking and pipe per kg.
Steel and pipe makers
Embrittlement resistant grades are now in service. Expect qualification demand.

Sources: BioEnergy Times, Nov 26, 2026; SMM, Nov 26, 2026

Regulation

UK cement and lime makers and unions sign a charter asking for carbon capture certainty

Producers Breedon, Tarmac, Holcim and Buxton Lime, unions GMB, Unite and the TUC, and the Mineral Products Association, the Global Cement and Concrete Association and the Carbon Capture and Storage Association signed the Cement and Lime Charter at a Westminster summit on Nov 25, Carbon Herald reported. Speakers said uneven carbon pricing and higher carbon imports hurt competitiveness. Peak Cluster says it would protect about 2,000 jobs and create 1,500 construction roles.

Policymakers
Industry and unions are asking together. Revenue model and timing decisions are the next step.
Builders
Domestic low carbon cement depends on capture. Factor it into long term supply plans.

Sources: Carbon Herald, Nov 26, 2026

Water

Nevada study says data centers could use 4 billion gallons of water a year by 2036

A Desert Research Institute report estimates Nevada's data centers could use 13,000 acre feet of water a year directly by 2036, plus 22,000 acre feet indirectly, Planetizen reported Nov 24, citing Facilities Dive. Nevada, the driest US state, has more than three dozen data centers with several dozen more planned. Data centers used 22% of the state's generation capacity in 2024, and the report estimates their 2036 use would equal all of Nevada's 2024 generation. Direct water use depends on cooling choices such as direct to chip and immersion.

Data center developers
Cooling choice sets water demand. Expect permits to ask for it.
Water utilities
Indirect use through power is larger than direct use. Plan with the power sector.

Sources: Planetizen via Facilities Dive, Nov 24, 2026

Projects

India and The Gambia add battery storage: L&T wins 900 MWh at Khavda, NVVN seeks 750 MWh

L&T won an order for a 300 MW, 900 MWh three hour battery at Khavda in Gujarat, SolarQuarter reported Nov 26. NVVN is seeking bids for 187.5 MW, 750 MWh in two projects, with viability gap funding capped at Rs 18 lakh per MWh or 30% of capital cost and bids due Dec 21, Power Peak Digest reported. The Gambia signed agreements for a $68 million, 52 MWp solar plant with a 40.9 MWh battery at Soma, with a 25 year PPA with NAWEC and about 101 GWh a year expected.

Suppliers
Indian tenders are moving to four hour systems. Price bids against the VGF cap.
Development lenders
West African solar plus storage is reaching signed PPAs. Watch financial close.

Sources: SolarQuarter, Nov 26, 2026; Power Peak Digest, Nov 26, 2026; SolarQuarter, Nov 26, 2026

NdRare earths

Lynas reports record NdPr output and an 88% jump in average selling price

Lynas produced a record 2,082 tonnes of NdPr in the September quarter and sales revenue rose 55% to A$310.1 million, with an average price of A$102.1 a kg across its products, Motley Fool Australia reported Nov 26. Shares rose about 7% to A$13.70. The company is targeting 900 tonnes of NdPr a month.

Sources: Motley Fool Australia, Nov 26, 2026

§Export controls

China keeps its gallium, germanium and antimony export ban to the US suspended to Jan 27

China's Commerce Ministry extended by two months, from Nov 28 through Jan 27, 2027, the suspension of its ban on US bound gallium, germanium, antimony and superhard materials, and kept stricter graphite reviews on hold, VOA reported Nov 26. Other controls remain. The US China trade truce expires Jan 10, 2027.

Sources: VOA, Nov 26, 2026

Qatar and the US sign a critical minerals and rare earths memorandum

The memorandum, signed in Washington alongside the eighth Qatar US Strategic Dialogue, covers mining and processing of critical minerals and rare earths and joint investment, Arab News reported Nov 25. No financial commitments, sites or timetables were disclosed.

Sources: Arab News, Nov 25, 2026

CuCopper

LME copper rebounds toward $12,000 from an 11 month low

Three month copper rose 2.65% to $11,958 a tonne early on Nov 26 after touching $12,089, Reuters reported, as proposed US tariffs on 14 trading partners were seen as unlikely to start before February or March. It settled at $11,820.58 on Nov 25, the lowest since December, and is still down more than 15% in November.

Sources: Reuters via Business Recorder, Nov 26, 2026; The Private Banker, Nov 26, 2026

TaTantalum

China's Xinhai to fund Arcadia's Namibia tantalum plant for up to 60% of the project

Xinhai Mining Services will fully fund construction and commissioning of the Swanson tantalum processing plant, designed for 15,000 tonnes a month, until a 30 day production test, Wisevoter reported Nov 27. The joint venture plans to pay out at least 70% of annual net profit as dividends.

Sources: Wisevoter, Nov 27, 2026

CGraphite

Molten Industries starts a plant making battery graphite and hydrogen from natural gas

The Oakland, California demonstration plant splits methane into graphite and hydrogen without combustion and can make 300 tonnes of anode graphite a year, the company said Nov 18. Samples are due from Dec 1. The company cites a US report that China controls 96% of battery graphite processing.

Sources: Business Wire via Batteries News, Nov 19, 2026
