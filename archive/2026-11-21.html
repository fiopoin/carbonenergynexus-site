Carbon markets

The EU and UK agree to link their carbon markets, with cross-border CO2 storage next in line

The deal sealed at the Brussels summit on November 20 would let EU and UK allowances count in both systems from 2028 and spare goods traded between them from each side's carbon border tax. The storage industry says North Sea CO2 shipping could get cheaper.

The EU and UK said at their summit in Brussels on November 20 that they had concluded negotiations to link their emissions trading systems. Allowances from each would be recognized in the other, and goods traded between the two would be exempt from each side's carbon border adjustment mechanism. Both sides aim to bring the link into effect in 2028, subject to legal procedures. The UK government said the link opens a route to waive EU CBAM charges on roughly £6.5 billion of UK goods.

The carbon deal is one of three agreements from the summit, which also covered a youth mobility scheme and food standards. The Irish Times, carrying the Financial Times report, said the EU's member states will only implement them if Britain agrees within six months to lower university fees for European students. Pre-summit reporting cited by Briefs Finance said the EU would recognize UK caps for 2028 to 2030, and from 2031 UK issuance would be limited to 7.1% of total EU ETS permits. On November 20 the UK December carbon contract traded at £58.40 a tonne and the EU benchmark at €81.85.

Storage is the industrial prize. Carbon Herald, citing industry analysis, reported that cross-border storage could cut offshore transport and storage costs for EU emitters by up to 28%, about €16 a tonne. The UK's Carbon Capture and Storage Association put the average saving at €11 a tonne and estimated £4 billion a year in UK export revenue and £7 billion of investment by 2040. It wants the EU to recognize storage in non-EU countries under the EU ETS by 2029. Critics such as MP Henry Tufnell have warned linkage could push UK carbon prices toward EU levels.

Sources: Carbon Herald, Nov 20, 2026; Briefs Finance, Nov 20, 2026; Irish Times (FT), Nov 20, 2026; Euronews, Nov 6, 2026; EC Joint Research Centre

Through the nexus

Electrons

UK power generators pay a lower carbon price than their EU peers today. A linked market would tend to close that gap, which feeds through to wholesale power prices on both sides.

Molecules

Refiners, chemical plants and gas fired generators on both sides would face one carbon signal, and UK exporters of steel, aluminium and fertilizer would escape EU border charges.

Pore space

The UK North Sea holds large depleted fields and saline aquifers. Recognition of cross-border storage would let EU emitters ship CO2 there and count it, which is where the €11 to €16 a tonne savings come from.

Water

More storage capacity means more capture, and capture is thirsty. A European Commission JRC assessment found adding CO2 capture raises a power plant's water use per unit of output by 50% to 62% for coal and gas combined cycle plants.

Use it

Investors
Price the spread between UK and EU allowances as a trade that should narrow toward 2028, but discount it for the six month condition attached to the summit package.
Regulators
Cross-border storage needs matching rules on liability and monitoring. Start aligning permits now so CO2 can move when the link goes live.
Industry
UK exporters to the EU should model their CBAM exposure with and without the waiver, since the waiver depends on linkage being completed.
Founders
Shipping, terminals and measurement for cross-border CO2 are the gaps the deal opens. Build for both regulators at once.

The brief

Capital

Finland's €300 million e-methane plant reaches a final investment decision

Nordic Ren-Gas said its Tampere project reached a final investment decision, which it calls the largest e-methane project to do so. The plant would make about 190 GWh a year of e-methane from renewable hydrogen and about 40,000 tonnes a year of biogenic CO2 captured from a local waste to energy plant, and send up to 200 GWh a year of process heat to Tampere's district heating network. Equity comes from Allianz, Tesi, Japan Hydrogen Fund and Schroders Greencoat; debt from the European Investment Bank, which lends €60 million under InvestEU, plus Société Générale, Standard Chartered, Nordea and Finnvera. The gas would move through the European grid to Germany, mainly for heavy trucks. Completion is scheduled for 2029.

Investors
The capital stack mixes insurers, a state fund and a Japanese hydrogen fund with export credit support. That template is worth copying for other e-fuel deals.
Industry
Waste to energy plants have a CO2 stream someone will pay for. Check whether yours qualifies as biogenic.

Sources: Hydrogen Central, Nov 21, 2026; Nordic Ren-Gas

Regulation

COP31 runs into overtime as fossil fuel and capture language divide countries

The Antalya talks missed their November 20 close. A late Friday plenary approved only technical matters, and a third draft was expected on Saturday, AFP reported. The latest text has no mention of fossil fuels and keeps a 35% electrification target for 2035, while some countries objected to its call to scale up carbon capture. A dozen European and developing countries, from France to Panama and the Solomon Islands, asked for a clear transition away from fossil fuels, and Panama has threatened to block a deal without it. Decisions need consensus. Australia's Chris Bowen, chairing the talks, said he would work for as long as it takes.

Developers
Do not plan around a COP capture number until it is adopted. National credits remain the economics.
Policy teams
Watch whether the final text names renewable electricity in the 35% goal, which some countries are asking for.

Sources: AFP via Dawn, Nov 21, 2026; AFP via Business Recorder, Nov 21, 2026; UN News, Nov 20, 2026

Regulation

Alberta pays carbon capture grants during construction, as Pathways papers slip

Alberta changed its Carbon Capture Incentive Program so some grants, worth 12% of eligible capital for certain oil and gas, petrochemical and power projects, are paid during construction rather than only after startup. The change aligns it with the federal CCUS tax credit. Energy and minerals minister Brian Jean said it will help accelerate projects. The Canadian Press noted that definitive agreements for the Pathways project, targeted for about November 15, have not been finalized. Pathways is now planned at about 6 million tonnes a year by the mid 2030s, down from an earlier 22 million tonnes by 2030.

Developers
Earlier cash lowers construction financing costs. Rerun project models with grant draws during the build.
Investors
Pathways is the bellwether. Signed fiscal agreements, not incentive tweaks, are what move it to FID.

Sources: Canadian Press via BNN Bloomberg, Nov 20, 2026

Regulation

Oregon and North Carolina make data centers carry more of their grid costs

Oregon regulators approved an agreement under which new Pacific Power data centers must pay their own energy costs from January 1, 2027, those above 100 MW pay a small surcharge for low income efficiency upgrades, and service can be curtailed in emergencies, according to a Sierra Club release. In North Carolina, the Utilities Commission created a rate schedule for Duke Energy customers of at least 50 MW with minimum contracts of 10 years, or 15 years at 100 MW or more, and demand charges on at least 75% of contracted demand, WRAL reported. Data centers make up 92% of the 6.7 GW Duke has contracted for new large customers.

Data center developers
Long contracts and exit fees are becoming standard. Budget for them in site selection.
Regulators
Both models protect households while keeping the door open. Compare them before writing your own tariff.

Sources: Sierra Club via CleanTechnica, Nov 19, 2026; WRAL, Nov 17, 2026

Water

Permian water networks change hands as treatment tries to beat the disposal well

Diamondback Energy and KKR agreed on November 10 to buy Five Point Infrastructure's 70% stake in Deep Blue, a Midland Basin water network that Diamondback says handles nearly 2 million barrels a day; Reuters, citing a source, put the price at about $2.1 billion, MarketScale reported. Closing is expected in the fourth quarter. In Reeves County, a plant near Orla treats up to 10,000 barrels a day of produced water by freezing rather than membranes, drawing 5.5 MW, Energies Media reported. It said Delaware Basin wells lift almost 5 barrels of water per barrel of oil, and that disposal costs about 60 cents a barrel against about $1.50 for reuse.

Investors
Water midstream is being valued like pipelines. Watch the gap between disposal and reuse costs, which decides growth.
Regulators
Injection pressure is building in the basin. Discharge permits for treated water are the policy lever to watch.

Sources: MarketScale, Nov 19, 2026; Energies Media, Nov 10, 2026

Critical minerals and mining

The metals behind grids, batteries, magnets and reactors. Supply sits in a few countries, so policy moves prices as much as demand does, and every mine and refinery is also a water and power project.

NdRare earths

US rare earth stocks slide again while Chinese offers rise

Critical Metals fell 8% to $3.21 by midday on Nov 20, and USA Rare Earth and MP Materials each fell about 5%, extending a selloff after a Nov 18 rally on hopes for US and China talks, 24/7 Wall St. reported. In China, SMM said Pr-Nd oxide offers were raised through the day as low priced material tightened, though buyers turned cautious. China Rare Nonferrous said prices were stable with moderate gains in 2026 and it is relatively optimistic about 2027.

Sources: 24/7 Wall St., Nov 20, 2026; SMM, Nov 20, 2026; SMM, Nov 20, 2026

CuCopper

LME copper breaks below $12,400 as Chinese premiums hit records

LME copper stood at $12,188 a tonne on the morning of Nov 20, SMM said, after breaking the $13,000 and $12,400 levels during the week, and it expects $12,000 to $12,500 next week. Three month copper fell 5.5% on Nov 18, its biggest one day drop since April 2025, while the Yangshan import premium hit a record, Crux Investor reported. LME stocks are near 330,000 tonnes.

Sources: SMM, Nov 20, 2026; Crux Investor, Nov 20, 2026

CoCobalt

China's cobalt imports jump 41% in October

China imported about 907 tonnes of unwrought cobalt in October, up 41% on September, led by Indonesia at 482 tonnes, and exported about 429 tonnes, down 19%, SMM reported Nov 20. January to October imports of 11,324 tonnes were nearly double last year. Domestic low end refined cobalt rebounded to 265,000 yuan a tonne by Nov 19 while Rotterdam fell to $18 a pound, turning exports unprofitable.

Sources: SMM, Nov 20, 2026

CGraphite

Anode prices hold, but cost support is fading

Artificial graphite anode prices stayed largely stable in the week to Nov 19 as coke prices fell and cell orders stayed sluggish, SMM said, warning prices could loosen if December battery schedules weaken. The suspension of China's anode export controls, extended to Jan 10, 2027, is prolonging a year end export rush.

Sources: SMM, Nov 19, 2026; Mysteel, Nov 11, 2026

NiNickel

Indonesia's state fund plans an export service fee

Danantara Sumberdaya Indonesia plans a single digit dollar per tonne service fee on commodity exports it supervises, starting with coal, palm oil and ferroalloys, its CEO said on Nov 13, with details due in December. Whether nickel products will be covered is not confirmed.

Sources: SMM, Nov 13, 2026

UUranium

Uranium holds near $90 a pound

US uranium futures traded above $91 a pound in November, the highest since February, and held near $90 around Nov 20, Trading Economics reported. Kazatomprom's September quarter sales turnover rose 25% on the year, and the IAEA counts 78 reactors under construction.

Sources: Trading Economics, Nov 2026
